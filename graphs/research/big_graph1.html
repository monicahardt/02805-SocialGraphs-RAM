Big Graph 1 · Bridge position and linguistic diversity

Do characters whose dialogue-network position makes them a bridge also use more varied language? The first plot places each character by their dialogue’s BERT embedding, projected into two dimensions with PaCMAP; color shows betweenness centrality on a shared logarithmic scale. Hover to see the original value. The second plot compares degree and betweenness.

Point size represents attributed dialogue word count.

BERT character embeddings · position = language, color = betweenness

Each character’s dialogue is represented by token-weighted mean sentence-transformer embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2), then projected together with PaCMAP. Nearby points have more similar BERT representations approximately; PaCMAP axes have no direct meaning. NetworkX already normalizes betweenness to 0–1; colors use a fixed logarithmic transform to make low values easier to distinguish. The scale is shared across books, movies, and edge rules; hover reports the original value.

Degree vs. betweenness, colored by utterance dispersion

Utterance dispersion is mean cosine distance from a speaker’s TF-IDF utterance centroid. It measures lexical/topic spread, not personality or all meanings of “linguistic diversity.”

Compare betweenness relative to degree: high degree alone can produce high betweenness. The network connects nearby attributed turns (or same-chapter speakers under that option), not confirmed addressees. This view is descriptive and does not include a degree-preserving null-model score.
