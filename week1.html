Fame and a long address book are different things.

Our question: does being widely referenced also mean referencing many other heroes? We counted incoming and outgoing links separately. Spider-Man leads incoming links with 106—35.0% of the 303-page roster. Betsy Braddock leads outgoing links with 28. The two rankings tell different stories.

An edge A → B means A’s Wikipedia article links to B’s article. In-degree counts the roster pages linking to a character; out-degree counts the characters that their page links to. These measure this snapshot’s article structure, not superpowers, friendship, or audience popularity.

Our interpretation: a widely recurring character can collect links from many separate articles, while a page covering many relationships or storylines may link out widely. The counts alone cannot establish why Betsy’s article leads; checking article text would be a useful follow-up.

A few hubs. A lot of quieter pages.

The average in-degree and out-degree are both 5.89: every directed edge contributes once to each total. Yet the maxima differ sharply—106 incoming versus 28 outgoing links.

The incoming distribution reaches much further than the outgoing one. A long tail is visible, but this small network and these plots do not establish a power law.

The universe has an outside.

Ignoring edge direction to find weakly connected components, we found one giant with 277 characters (91.4%), a separate nine-character island, and 17 singletons. That second island surprised us: everything outside the giant is not simply an isolated page.

The 17 isolates

“Isolated” means no incoming or outgoing links within this roster and frozen snapshot. It does not mean these characters have no Wikipedia links or no place in Marvel’s stories. Loading only the edge list would erase all 17 of them.

How we counted

We loaded every node from the roster first, then added the directed edges. We checked unique node IDs, duplicate edges, and that every edge endpoint appears in the roster. We counted degrees and traversed the undirected neighbor relationships to find weak components.
