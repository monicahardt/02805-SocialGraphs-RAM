Weekly writeup

Working with AI is a very iterative process, with a lot of back and forth, so there is not a clear "list of questions" asked. We first asked a lot of questions about setting up the github page, navigating between pages and general project scaffolding questions. We then started working on the visualizations. Codex had just released their new GPT 6 Astra model, which we just let run amok at first with very vague prompts. While the graphs produced were decent, it gave a big overhead in analyzing and trying to understand them. They were also written in .js as we hadn't specified the format, so we got them translated to .jpynb instead. Next week we will likely take a more cautious approach, with smaller iterations and more precise prompts, to more easily grasp the visualizaitons made.

      06 / Community discovery

      A living atlas

      One possible grouping of Wikipedia pages. These communities are inferred from links; membership can change with the random seed. They are not verified Marvel teams.

      • Community bubble area: number of characters. “C2” means Community 2; the larger number is its member count. Once you open the individual characters, their sizes reflect incoming links.
      • Bubble position: arranged to fit the screen. Distance between bubbles does not measure relatedness.

      Does another seed give the same groups?

      The map stays on seed 42. Agreement compares pairs of characters placed together in either run; 100% means identical top-level membership. It is a sensitivity check across five seeds, not a confidence score. Group IDs in different runs are arbitrary.

      Members in this view
        Characters with the most neighbors in other communities
          Detection and animation rules

          Isolates are grouped for display only. Simulated visitors follow a uniformly chosen outgoing link with probability 85%; otherwise they jump uniformly to an active character. A page with no outgoing link always restarts. Jumps draw no links. Opening a group restarts visitors inside that view; they can then leave it. At character level, node size grows with original in-degree.

          This is by far the most interesting visualization "visually" that we were able to make. It separates the heroes into communities, based on how the characters link to each other, with a "leader" assigned to each (also based on link amount). You can enter into a community to see how the characters link together. Once inside a community, you can also use the button below the visualization to find the characters on the Network Lab visualization. But the best part of the visualization is the simulation. In the top right, you can select simulate "random clicks" (walkers) across wikipedia articles. The visualization then shows how these clicks flow across communities. Clicking into a community will show them inside the communities (note they will disapear if they enter a link outside the community)
